## Project context you must preserve

Bestehen / Corpus is the exam-data and intelligence product behind Bestehen / App. It gathers and structures broad German A2/B1 exam evidence, preserves provenance, confidence, rights, quality and independence semantics, and publishes eligible pattern intelligence to the learner-facing App. The App owns learner state, coaching, Readiness, and original-practice generation/evaluation.

This is initially a **single-owner personal project**. Architecture must therefore be cheap to operate, easy to understand and replay, and deliberately resistant to enterprise-style overbuilding.

Six truth axes must remain independent in every later data model, aggregate, index, API, cache and UI state: source/provenance; authenticity/evidence confidence; rights/usage; difficulty plus Generic-B1/TELC relevance; extraction/QA readiness by capability; and content identity/derivative lineage/metric-specific analytical independence. Unknown/disputed/review-required/incomplete/provisional states are first-class. Publication is capability-scoped; there is no universal `production_ready=true`.

The normal Corpus → App contract is **pattern-first and learner-data-free**. Corpus must not receive learner profiles/attempts/Readiness, and ordinary App sessions must not trigger live Corpus inference. Every external source/file/media/model input is untrusted. The cost posture is deterministic/local/cheap-first, with measured escalation and bounded paid work.

## Product/design decisions you must not reopen

Do not redefine Corpus as a learner product, public mirror or generic scraper. Do not merge confidence with rights, source volume with independence, or QA with trust. Do not introduce learner data, a universal ready flag, live per-session Corpus inference, automatic publication from model output, active source execution, or speculative internet-scale infrastructure.

Treat design as authoritative for observable states/safety/accessibility, not as a backend/runtime mandate.
