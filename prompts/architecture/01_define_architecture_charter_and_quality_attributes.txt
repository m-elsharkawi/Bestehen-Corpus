## Project context you must preserve

Bestehen / Corpus is the exam-data and intelligence product behind Bestehen / App. It gathers and structures broad German A2/B1 exam evidence, preserves provenance, confidence, rights, quality and independence semantics, and publishes eligible pattern intelligence to the learner-facing App. The App owns learner state, coaching, Readiness, and original-practice generation/evaluation.

This is initially a **single-owner personal project**. Architecture must therefore be cheap to operate, easy to understand and replay, and deliberately resistant to enterprise-style overbuilding.

Six truth axes must remain independent in every later data model, aggregate, index, API, cache and UI state:

1. source/provenance;
2. authenticity/evidence confidence;
3. rights/usage;
4. difficulty plus Generic-B1/TELC relevance;
5. extraction/QA readiness by capability;
6. content identity, derivative lineage and metric-specific analytical independence.

`unknown`, `not_applicable`, `disputed`, `review_required`, `unresolved_dependency`, `incomplete`, `provisional` and equivalent explicit non-final states are first-class. Evidence is local: a trusted parent cannot rescue a defective child, and one local defect must not invalidate unrelated sibling capabilities. Publication is capability-scoped; there is no universal `production_ready=true`.

The normal Corpus → App contract is **pattern-first and learner-data-free**. Corpus must not receive learner profiles/attempts/Readiness, and ordinary App sessions must not trigger live Corpus inference. Every external source/file/media/model input is untrusted. The cost posture is deterministic/local/cheap-first, with measured escalation and bounded paid work.

## Architecture decisions this stage owns

Settle and document all of the following, plus every Stage 01 checklist checkbox:

1. **Architecture principles.** Define a small explicit set that preserves product boundary, six-axis separation, uncertainty/locality, capability publication, immutable observations, append-only audit, replayability, replaceability and one-owner maintainability.
2. **Quality-attribute scenarios.** Turn product goals into measurable scenarios for correctness, provenance integrity, rights safety, security, recoverability, availability/degradation, performance, owner operability, accessibility, benchmark qualification and cost. Each scenario should state stimulus, environment, expected response and measurable response measure.
3. **Architecture budgets.** Translate the roughly 20k–50k canonical Item design center, approximately `€3 / 1,000 Items` variable-processing target and approximately `€25/month` normal Corpus cash envelope into architectural budgets/guardrails without hardcoding empirically recalibratable product numbers.
4. **Decision precedence.** State how later stages decide when simplicity, quality, evidence, rights, security, App compatibility, cost and owner effort conflict.
5. **Forbidden shortcuts.** Explicitly ban designs such as one ready flag, rights=trust coupling, destructive rerun, learner-data ingress, live App search/inference dependency, unbounded retries/paid loops, active source execution, mirror-count prevalence, and speculative internet-scale infrastructure.
6. **Version/audit vocabulary.** Define the minimum cross-stage language for stable identity, mutable revision, source observation, method/model/rule/taxonomy/contract versions, findings, holds, stale, provisional and superseded states. Do not design the physical schema yet.
7. **Graceful-degradation doctrine.** Unsafe work stops; expensive work may defer; known-valid outputs survive; uncertainty stays visible; infrastructure/provider failure never becomes corpus truth.
8. **Research policy.** Define which mutable architecture decisions require current primary official sources and how access dates/evidence must be recorded.
9. **ADR policy.** Confirm the lightweight ADR convention and criteria for creating/superseding ADRs.
10. **Cross-stage proof obligations.** Define what every later artifact must say about failure/recovery, security/privacy/rights, performance/reliability/observability, cost and testing.

## Failure, security, cost and testing requirements

Your charter must make clear that:

- failure/partial/blocked/provisional states are architecture states, not exceptional prose;
- source/model content is data, never authority;
- rights and security holds override ordinary processing paths without mutating other axes;
- human review time is a cost and repeated failure/reprocessing must be measured;
- benchmark qualification is required before automated production claims;
- later tests must prove invariants, not merely happy paths.
