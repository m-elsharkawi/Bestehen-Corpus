## Define Data Ownership, Persistence, Storage, and Schema Evolution

You are the data architect responsible for **Stage 05: Data Ownership, Persistence, Storage, and Schema Evolution**.

Your job is to choose persistence/storage boundaries that preserve raw observations, curated decisions, derived intelligence, revisions, replay, rights/takedown semantics, audit and cost without premature polyglot infrastructure. This is **architecture decision work, not production implementation**. Do not create production migrations, DDL, ORM code or cloud resources.

## Project context

Corpus turns untrusted German A2/B1 source evidence into versioned rights-aware intelligence for Bestehen / App. It is initially a one-owner personal project. Six axes—provenance, authenticity/confidence, rights, difficulty/relevance, capability QA readiness, and identity/metric independence—must remain separately queryable and versioned. Observed, curated and derived state are not interchangeable. Unknown/local/provisional states are first-class. App receives pattern-first publication and sends no learner data. Cheap, replayable, content-reusing storage is preferred.

## Decisions not to reopen

Do not flatten six axes into one row/status. Do not overwrite observed source text with corrections. Do not delete duplicate observations to save space. Do not assume rights/takedown means all evidence must be physically erased identically; Stage 04 product rights rules govern what is retained/suppressed/purged/recomputed. Do not introduce a warehouse/lakehouse/vector/search store before a measured need.

## Decisions this stage owns

1. **Primary persistence strategy.** Choose the principal durable store and justify whether relational/document capabilities are primary. State why additional search/vector/graph stores are or are not needed at launch.
2. **Logical data stores.** Define authoritative records vs projections/caches/artifact/blob storage and their owning bounded contexts.
3. **Identity and revision persistence.** Define stable IDs, immutable source observations, mutable revisions/interpretations, derived version lineage and referential rules across reprocessing.
4. **Observed/curated/derived separation.** Specify persistence patterns that make edits append/supersede rather than erase source observations.
5. **Transactional boundaries.** Define what must commit atomically and what can reach eventual consistency/stale state safely.
6. **Content addressing.** Decide how immutable source/artifact/media bytes and repeated processing outputs are fingerprinted/reused, including collision/integrity considerations and takedown fingerprint interactions.
7. **Schema evolution.** Define migration/version strategy for structural schema, taxonomy references, processing versions, analytics materializations and App contract snapshots.
8. **Retention/deletion/suppression.** Define classes for backed-up durable truth, reproducible derivatives, safe ephemera/cache, suppressed content and physical deletion. Reconcile audit/provenance preservation with rights/security/privacy deletion requirements.
9. **Backup/restore foundation.** Define what must be backed up vs can be regenerated and restoration ordering; Stage 24 owns operational drill/monitoring details.
10. **Query needs.** Prove the model can support owner Inbox/detail/audit, capability eligibility, reprocessing, analytics and App publication without denormalizing away semantics.
